Figure 1. Fractional quotas, realized cutoffs, and score spacing.
Pre-award academic scores range from 0 to 100. Each programme awards scholarships to the top 10% of applicants. Independent uniform scores have population reference cutoff 90. The realized cutoff is the midpoint of the last selected and first unselected scores. Panel (a) reports its Monte Carlo RMSE relative to 90, with the exact finite-sample benchmark including bias. Panel (b) reports the mean adjacent score gap, with exact expectation 100/(m+1). Both axes in both panels are logarithmic. There are 4,000 replications per size.

Figure 2. Local linear RD as the number of applicants per programme increases.
Each programme awards scholarships to the top 10% of applicants. Without a scholarship, the subsequent academic performance index equals 40 + 0.3 times the pre-award score plus independent Gaussian noise with standard deviation 3. Receiving the scholarship raises the index by 5 points. Separate local linear fits use triangular weights at the realized midpoint and h = 0.5 m^(-1/4) on scores divided by 100. Panel (a) gives the Monte Carlo mean and central 95% range across replications; the shaded band is not a confidence interval from one sample. Panel (b) reports coverage of nominal 95% normal intervals using HC0 sandwich standard errors. Error bars are 1.96 Monte Carlo standard errors. There are 4,000 replications per size.

Figure 3. More small programmes improve precision without removing the baseline difference.
Each independent programme selects 1 of its 10 applicants by pre-award score. The estimator averages the observed outcome difference between ranks 1 and 2. Under the common model, its expectation is 5 + 30/11 = 7.7273. The scholarship effect is 5 index points. Panel (a) shows the Monte Carlo mean and central 95% range. Panel (b) evaluates the same nominal 95% normal interval against the outcome-difference target and the causal effect. The standard error is the sample SD of the programme-level differences divided by the square root of the number of programmes. Error bars show 1.96 Monte Carlo standard errors. There are 4,000 replications per design. The designs with more programmes extend the same sequence of draws. Horizontal axes are logarithmic.
