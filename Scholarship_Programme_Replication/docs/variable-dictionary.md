# Variables and units

`data/raw/cluster_mc.csv.gz`: one row per cluster-size design and repetition. `m` is applicants per cluster, `k` is selected applicants, and `repetition` identifies the Monte Carlo draw. `cutoff` and `gap` are in normalized [0,1] score units. `estimate`/`se` are the local linear effect and HC0 standard error in outcome units; nonlinear counterparts use the paired nonlinear model. `n_plus`/`n_minus` count positive-weight observations in the two windows. RD fields are intentionally missing at sizes below the declared RD minimum.

`data/raw/small_cluster_mc.csv.gz` contains one row per independent-cluster count G and repetition. `m=10`, `k=1`, `estimate` is the mean outcome difference between ranks 1 and 2, and `se` is the sample SD of cluster-level differences divided by sqrt(G). The outcome is measured in academic performance index points.

`data/raw/illustrative_applicants.csv` contains separate full-data examples for 10 and 1,000 applicants. `cluster_size` gives the number of students applying to the programme, `applicant` identifies the student within the example, and `rank` orders students by descending pre-award `score`. `selected` equals 1 for scholarship recipients and 0 for other applicants. The score and realized `cutoff` are on the 0-100 pre-award scale. `potential_y0` and `potential_y1` give the simulated subsequent academic performance indices without and with a scholarship. `observed_y` gives the index under the student's assigned award status. These examples are separate from the Monte Carlo draws used for the figures.

Summary tables report MC means, SDs, RMSEs, confidence-interval coverage, and Monte Carlo standard errors. `q025`/`q975` are empirical estimator quantiles, not confidence limits for one sample. `target` is the population object used to assess coverage. The cutoff table uses normalized units; plots and guide multiply by the configured score scale. Failed-fit counts and minimum/median window counts are retained. Analytic sensitivity varies the known simulated untreated slope.
